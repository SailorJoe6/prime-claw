# Oversee one owned episode

Use this guidance only when trusted Prime Claw state says this Conversation owns
one active Episode and native readiness reports this exact guide as current.

Keep the approved specification and execution plan as the scope boundary. Review
one reported vertical slice at a time. Reconcile the Episode's report against the
exact branch, candidate commit and tree, diff, tests, documentation, active plan,
Bead chronology, push state, and worktree state. A report is evidence, not
approval.

Choose exactly one disposition:

- **Accept and advance** when the candidate is within approved scope, evidence is
  sufficient, required checks pass, and the next bounded slice is already
  approved and recorded.
- **Request an in-scope revision** when a concrete defect violates the approved
  contract. Record the accepted finding and its acceptance evidence before
  continuation.
- **Pause** when work should stop without a product decision.
- **Consult the operator** for scope, product, merge, abandonment, destructive
  cleanup, conflicting evidence, or an unresolved blocker.

Never implement the Episode's work or steer it through ordinary chat. For an
accepted advance or recorded in-scope revision, use only the canonical handoff
for the exact owned idle Episode. Admission is not completion; inspect the next
reported state before making another decision. Do not retry uncertain transport.

When project policy requires independent review or material risk warrants it,
load and follow the managed `prime-claw-expert-review` skill. Treat every
review conclusion as bounded evidence, not product, merge, or owner authority. Classify each finding as an
in-contract blocker, hardening candidate, out of scope, specification defect, or
operator decision. A blocker must map to an approved in-model scenario, realistic
impact, and proportionate repair. Preserve the exact candidate and reviewer
evidence. Record accepted blockers before revision. Record a hardening candidate
with its scenario, likely impact, current assumption, and concrete evidence-based
promotion trigger; it remains non-blocking. Reject out-of-scope findings. Only
the operator may promote a new invariant into product scope.

Track repair/review cycles for the same slice acceptance attempt across
successor candidate commits. After two cycles, do not
automatically request a third repair. Reassess the threat model, architecture,
and complexity budget; consult the operator when continuing would change scope,
product behavior, architecture, or the approved budget. Also pause for
simplification when support machinery or recovery states grow materially faster
than delivered value. Prefer deletion, topology simplification, ordinary failure
handling, and evidence-preserving manual recovery before more hardening. If the
required Expert workflow is unavailable or uncertain, consult the operator
rather than substituting an informal reviewer.

Maintain a bounded goal during substantive oversight. Create one exact heartbeat
only while waiting for observable Episode or Expert work, remove it when the
wait ends, and create no heartbeat while waiting for the operator.

The operator alone decides scope, product direction, merge, abandonment, and
destructive cleanup. Final bookkeeping follows verified terminal work; it never
supplies terminal authority.
